# Content style guide

These rules apply to everything on the hub, in issues and in pull requests. Several are enforced by self-checks in the page; the check numbers are given where they apply.

## Sources and evidence
- **Read the source in full.** If only a summary or abstract could be read, say so on the point.
- **Two independent sources, at least one authoritative,** for every figure and every claim that could be disputed. Authoritative means official statistics, regulators, government, peer-reviewed research, recognised archives, or the organisation itself for its own facts. Two pages from one publisher count as one. **Wikipedia is never counted.**
- **Quote exactly.** Each source on a point carries the short quote that supports it; every source link must use https (C03).
- **Never fabricate.** If a fact cannot be confirmed, leave it out or label it "not confirmed by the sources cited".
- **Disputed points** are marked disputed and give both figures, with who published each.
- **Our own calculations** (a sum, a percentage) say so, and how they were made.
- **Show strengths as well as gaps,** and the strongest counter-argument where the hub makes an argument.

## People and privacy
- Name only people who have died, or living people in their public role.
- **Never classify any individual's ethnicity.** Use counts published by the awarding body, a statistics body or a reputable independent analysis.
- No personal data about the maker, contributors or anyone else: no contact details, private links or private project names (C157, `tests/privacy_scan.py`).
- No team email address on the hub.

## Language
- UK English and GOV.UK style: plain words, short sentences, sentence case headings, numerals for numbers.
- Capital B for Black. Use specific groups (Black African, Black Caribbean, Mixed White and Black Caribbean) rather than broad labels.
- Do not use "BAME", "BME" or "non-white" in the hub's own words. If a source uses them, quote and attribute them ("the source's 'BAME' grouping"). The hub's language check (C123) enforces this.
- Historical terms are explained, not silently removed: the **Language of the time** section explains terms such as "half-caste" or "coloured" when they appear in quotations or names (C158). Works written to spread prejudice are paraphrased, not quoted at length.
- "Enslaved people", not "slaves"; "enslaver", not "slave owner".

## Structure
- Long pages open with **In short** (three to six sentences, each no longer than 25 words, every number traceable to a linked point; C146).
- **From where you stand** gives suggestions for five readers: Black, African, Caribbean or Mixed readers; colleagues and allies; leaders; policy makers and service designers; data and analysis professionals.
- Menus hold at most nine pages. New content joins the page it belongs with.
- Coverage should be fair around the world, not only the UK: the global coverage audit (C159) sets floors for under-covered regions.

## Accessibility
- WCAG 2.2 AA at 390px and 1280px, light and dark.
- Every chart has a table; every image has a text alternative; link text makes sense on its own.
- Do not rely on colour alone.
