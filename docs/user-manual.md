# User manual

The BHM 2026 Hub brings together Black and Black-Mixed history, heritage and cultures, Africa and the African diaspora, for Black History Month 2026 and all year. It was built for colleagues across the UK public sector and the communities they serve, and anyone can use it. Every figure links to a source that was read.

This manual explains how to open the hub, find your way, use its tools and take part. Inside the hub, **About → User guide and tours** has guided tours and step-by-step tutorials.

## 1. Ways to open the hub
| Way | How | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Website | https://cyberdudeuk.github.io/black-mixed-heritage/ | Everyday use on any device |
| Single file | Download `dist/bhm-2026-hub.html` from the repository and open it in a browser | Offline use, sharing as an attachment, places where websites are blocked |
| Microsoft Teams | Your Teams administrator, or you, can add the app in `teams/` as a tab | Teams channels and meetings |
| Inside Claude | If the hub has been shared with you in Claude | Saving club votes, the shared ideas box, the AI assistant |

All of them hold the same content. Saving votes, shared ideas and the AI assistant work only inside Claude; elsewhere the hub says so and offers an alternative.

## 2. Finding your way
- **Menus:** seven menus across the top (on phones, one **Menu** button). Each menu has its own colour.
- **Search:** select **Search** or press `/` from anywhere. It finds sections, headings and every listed item. Try "jollof", "Windrush", "Wales", "patent" or "reparations".
- **Contents:** the **Contents** switch opens the list of sections on the page you are on, with counts and flags for what is **new** or **updated** since your last visit. You can filter the list.
- **Related pages, previous and next:** at the end of every page.
- **A to Z and knowledge graph:** **About → Knowledge layer** lists every term and item and shows how they connect.
- **Links to a section:** every section has its own address, so you can share a link straight to it.
- **Guided tours:** **About → User guide and tours** has five tours: First visit, Plan an event, For leaders, For schools and educators, and Can I trust this?

## 4. What is on every page
- **In short:** long pages open with a few plain-English sentences. Each links to the evidence behind it.
- **At a glance:** reference pages open with a summary worked out from their own data.
- **Sourced points:** each fact shows its source, the quote that supports it, the period and place it covers, and any caveat. "Disputed" means sources disagree, and both are given.
- **From where you stand:** suggestions for different readers: Black, African, Caribbean or Mixed readers; colleagues and allies; leaders; policy makers and service designers; data and analysis professionals.
- **Filters:** many pages let you show points about one intersection (for example women and gender, faith, children and young people, health and disability, migration and the diaspora), or filter by type, era and order.
- **Charts:** every chart has **Show the data as a table**.
- **Language of the time:** historical terms that appear in quotations or names are underlined and explained.
- **Sources for this page:** at the end of each page, everything that was read, and when.

## 5. Accessibility settings
Use the buttons at the top right:
- **Theme** switches light and dark.
- **Access** offers text size (100%, 112%, 125%, 150%), a highly legible font, more space between lines and letters, underlined links, high contrast, and whether to play animations. Animations stay off if your device asks for reduced motion. **Reset all** clears them. Settings are saved in your browser only.

Keyboard: `Tab` moves through links and buttons, and the first link skips to the content; `/` opens search; `Escape` closes search, menus and tooltips. Tooltips stay open while you hover over them and close with `Escape`.

To report a barrier, see [section 7](#7-ideas-wishes-corrections-and-bugs).

## 6. Things you can do
| Task | Where | How |
|---|---|---|
| Add an event to your calendar | This month → Calendar | Choose an event, then Google Calendar or Outlook, or download the `.ics` file. To subscribe to the whole calendar, add `https://cyberdudeuk.github.io/black-mixed-heritage/data/bhm2026-events.ics` as an internet calendar. Award and nomination deadlines are included (filter **Nomination deadlines**), and subscribed calendars remind you 14 days and 2 days before each one |
| Suggest an event for the calendar | GitHub: the **Event for the calendar** form | Write the dates as YYYY-MM-DD and give the organiser's public page. Once a maintainer accepts it, it is added through a reviewed change and appears in the calendar, marked as suggested by the community |
| Follow what is new | Any feed reader | Add `https://cyberdudeuk.github.io/black-mixed-heritage/data/bhm2026-releases.atom` |
| Plan an event | This month → Organiser toolkit | Checklist and activity ideas filtered by setting, effort and format |
| Make a poster | This month → Posters | Type your event's title, date and place once; every poster updates. Choose a palette, then download or copy. Add alternative text when you share it |
| Download templates | Resources → Brand and media | Slide, report and induction pack templates |
| Run a book or film club | This month → Book and film club | Filter by type, genre and audience; read the content notes; open the discussion guide; download the meeting dates |
| Find evidence for a briefing | Lead → Inequalities and action | Read "At a glance", open the domain you need, and open each source before quoting it. Benchmarks shows how the UK compares |
| Recognise colleagues | This month → Get involved | Nomination routes for honours and awards, with deadlines |
| Learn with children or students | Resources → For schools and educators | Curriculum links, resources, quizzes and family games by age |
| Check a myth | Understand → Myths and facts | Each statement is marked true or false, with sources |
| Ask the hub (inside Claude) | **Ask** at the top | Answers only from the hub's content, with sources; can draft text, correct a myth or fill the poster form. Outside Claude, use **Find in the hub** in the same panel |

## 7. Ideas, wishes, corrections and bugs
Go to **About → Help run the hub**.
- **Ideas box:** choose a type (idea, feature wish, bug, correction or accessibility barrier), give a short title and details, and add it. Do not include personal information. To share it with everyone, select **send it to GitHub**: this opens the right GitHub form already filled in, including the page you were on.
- **Ideas and wishes on GitHub:** on the website, the same page lists the ideas and feature wishes raised on GitHub, with how many people support each one and its status (new, accepted, planned, in progress, done, declined). To support one, open it on GitHub and add a thumbs-up reaction. The list updates within a few hours.
- **Cite, link or correct a single point:** every sourced point has **Cite, link or correct** at its foot: a permanent link, a citation with the version to copy, and **Suggest a correction on GitHub**, which opens the correction form already filled in with the point.
- **On GitHub:** buttons to report a bug, correct a fact or source, report an accessibility barrier, suggest an idea or request a feature. You need a free GitHub account. Issues are public.
- **Champion pack:** words to copy for your intranet, a team message and a social post, and six steps to share the hub in your organisation, profession or network.
- **Record your contribution:** keep a log of what you do for the hub, with hours and the Civil Service Success Profiles behaviours, for objectives or CPD. It stays in your browser; save it as a text file or copy it.
- **Thanks, and what changed:** contributors who chose to be named, and on the website the suggestions that became changes.
- **Volunteer:** the same page sets out the roles that keep the hub going (now including champions and ambassadors, and field review panels), and has a form to express interest. Inside Claude, your answers are saved privately. Elsewhere, the form offers to open GitHub's **Volunteer** form with your roles, months, skills and commitment filled in; it does not copy your notes or networks, because GitHub issues are public.

## 8. Can I trust it?
- Every figure links to a source that was opened and read, with the date it was read. Where possible there are two independent sources, at least one authoritative. Wikipedia is not counted as a source.
- Disputed figures are marked, and both are shown.
- **Assurance → What changed** lists the items added, changed or removed since the previous release; cards that are new or changed carry a label. **About → Knowledge layer → Open data and API** links every open data file, including every sourced point.
- The hub never classifies any person's ethnicity: it uses counts published by others.
- The hub checks itself: **About → Self-checks, stories and journeys** shows 169 checks that run in your browser each time you open it, including sources, accessibility, language, privacy and coverage. Each check has a deliberate-break test proving it can fail.
- **About → Review and assessment** and **Assurance** set out quality scores and what still needs work. The **Coverage map** on Review and assessment shows how many sourced points the hub holds for each field, design discipline, government function, Civil Service profession and part of the public sector, and what is being added next. On the website, **Assurance → Repository checks** shows the latest result of each check that runs on every change in the public repository, and **Source freshness** lists every source with the date it was last read and when it is due to be read again.
- It is unofficial, made in a personal capacity, and not endorsed by Black History Month UK or HM Government.
