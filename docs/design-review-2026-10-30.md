# Design and information architecture review, 30 October 2026

The hub measured all 61 pages at 1280px light and 390px dark against the GOV.UK Service Standard and Design System, GOV.UK content design, Nielsen's usability heuristics, WCAG 2.2, typographic measure, Hick's and Fitts's laws, Gestalt grouping and the hub's DEEIA lens. Findings marked Done are kept fixed by self-checks C172 and C177; findings marked Recommended carry the measure that will show they worked. The same tables are on the hub's Review and assessment page.

## Design review

| Area | Framework | What was measured | Done or recommended | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Structure | WCAG 2.2 (1.3.1), GOV.UK Design System headings | 36 pages jumped a heading level. 'In short' was a level 3 heading straight after the page title, the calendar month was a level 3, the champion texts used level 4 under level 2, and on 15 pages the first section's heading became the page title, leaving its cards with no section heading | 'In short' and the calendar month are level 2 and the champion texts level 3, with their look unchanged; where a first section's heading becomes the page title, a visually hidden heading names the section; C177 fails on any skipped level | Done (C177) |
| Layout | WCAG 2.2 (1.4.10 Reflow) | Two pages scrolled sideways at 390px: the unfair treatment ladder (long words in a grid cell) and the champion texts on Help run the hub (preformatted text that never wrapped) | Grid cells may shrink and words wrap; the champion texts wrap; C177 fails if any page is wider than the screen | Done (C177) |
| Typography | Typographic measure (45 to 75 characters a line), GOV.UK two-thirds column | At 1280px, paragraphs ran to about 116 characters a line on most pages and up to 174 | Paragraphs and 'In short' lists are capped at about 75 characters; C177 fails above 90 | Done (C177) |
| Typography | GOV.UK Design System type scale, WCAG 1.4.4 | Map hints and band captions were 11.2px to 11.5px, and source notes set in the monospace font 12.5px | Raised to 13px, the smallest size used for running text elsewhere; C177 fails below 13px | Done (C177) |
| Accessibility | WCAG 2.2 (3.1.1 Language of page) | The single-file edition could open with no page language, so screen readers might guess | The page sets British English when none is given; C177 checks it | Done (C177) |
| Navigation | Nielsen: recognition rather than recall; Gestalt proximity | 21 pages had no topic signpost, including Culture, Food, Innovation, Futures, the diaspora and the housekeeping pages | Three new signposts (culture, ideas and the future; learning and reference; about this hub) and two pages added to existing ones: every page except the Overview now names its siblings | Done (C172) |
| Content | Order of precedence (most important first) | On 40 pages the topic signpost came between the page title and its In short summary, pushing the summary below the first screen | In short now comes first and the signpost follows it; C177 fails if a signpost comes first | Done (C177) |
| Navigation | Hick's law, GOV.UK navigation | 8 menus, at most 9 pages each, 60 pages placed once; This month and Lead now hold 8 | Keep every menu at 9 or fewer; review if a menu reaches 9 again | Holding (C172) |
| Content | GOV.UK content design: plain English | Median sentence 19.6 words; on 12 pages more than 30% of sentences exceed 25 words (Assurance and Self-checks 47%, Benchmarks 44%); Flesch reading ease median 44, below 30 on Calendar, Talent, Benchmarks, Stereotypes, Design, Review and Assurance | Rewrite the longest sentences on those 12 pages first, starting with the reader-facing ones (Benchmarks, Stereotypes, Profession, Leaders); keep technical pages technical but lead each with a plain summary. Measure: under 20% of sentences over 25 words on every reader-facing page | Recommended |
| Content | Progressive disclosure, Nielsen: aesthetic and minimalist design | Assurance is about 84,000 words and 750,000px tall at 390px; Knowledge layer about 34,000; ten pages exceed 15,000 words | Open long registers collapsed with a count and a filter, and split Assurance into tabs (checks, sources, what changed); keep every item reachable by search and anchor. Measure: no page taller than 60 screens at 390px before a reader opens anything | Recommended |
| Content | UCD: In short and From where you stand | All 40 content pages open with an In short summary and close with From where you stand; the 21 without are tools and housekeeping pages, by design | Keep both on every new content page; C138 to C146 already check them | Holding |
| Interaction | WCAG 2.5.8 (met), 2.5.5 AAA and GOV.UK 44px touch targets | No axe target-size failures, but 124 controls under 24px remained at 390px: tick boxes of 13px to 20px (Help run the hub, Organiser toolkit, Involve, Brand), text fields 21px high, small buttons 23px high and the History year slider | Tick boxes are 24px everywhere; on touch screens text fields and lists are 44px high and sliders 24px; small buttons are at least 24px; C177 fails if a visible tick box or button on the page is under 24px | Done (C177) |
| Performance | GOV.UK Service Standard 14 (operate a reliable service), Core Web Vitals | One page of about 152,000 elements and 21.6MB as rendered; the self-checks finish about 14 to 15 seconds after opening from a file | Render each page's sections when the page is first opened rather than all at start-up, and load the bibliography lists on demand; Measure: interactive in under 5 seconds on a mid-range phone | Recommended |
| Graphic | GOV.UK Design System colour, WCAG 1.4.3 and 1.4.11 | axe-core on every page at both themes finds no contrast failures after the fixes in this release; 20 type sizes are in use on a page | Reduce the type scale to the design tokens (about 8 sizes) when pages are next touched; Measure: no more than 10 distinct sizes | Recommended |
| Visualisation | Data storytelling, show the data as a table | Charts appear on 13 pages, each with a table view; most pages tell their story in text and cards | Add a single 'headline chart' to reader-facing pages that carry trend figures (Leaders, Representation, Recognition), each with what it means, what the publisher says and what to be careful about | Recommended |
| DEEIA | The hub's DEEIA lens: diversity, equity, equality, inclusion, accessibility | Nobody's ethnicity is classified; sources are dated and two-sourced; perspectives for Black, African, Caribbean and Mixed readers, allies, leaders, designers and analysts on every content page; access settings for text size, spacing, contrast and motion; language of the time explained | Hold these; next, test the hub with Black and Mixed-heritage users with access needs (screen reader, magnifier, cognitive), as the GOV.UK Service Standard asks (point 1, 2 and 5), and publish what changed | Recommended |
| UCD | GOV.UK Service Standard 1 to 3, Double Diamond | Requirements come from people's questions (R95 to R106), but no usability sessions have yet been run with the hub's audiences | Run five moderated sessions per audience (organisers, leaders, people joining government, young people) on the top tasks in the user guide; record task success and time, and feed findings into the next release | Recommended |
