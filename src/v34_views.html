Identity, not biology

What it means to be Black: around the world, and the African context in the UK

What 'Black' means as an identity

There is no single answer to what it means to be Black. It is an identity people define for themselves, shaped by history, place, politics and law, not by biology. This page sets out how the meaning changes around the world, who the Black and African communities of the UK are, and what research reports about their lives. Every point links to its source.

      Five words, five different things

      The DEEIA lens: diversity, equity, equality, inclusion and accessibility

      What the five terms mean

      Diversity, equity, equality, inclusion and accessibility (DEEIA) are five different things, often run together. This page says what each means, what UK law requires, what government policy has been since 2022, what is measured, and where people disagree, with the strongest case on each side. It then reads every new page of the hub through all five, so you can see which dimensions each covers. Every point links to its source.

      Show points about:

      At a glance

      The five dimensions at a glance

      Short working definitions used on this page. The sourced detail, including where the terms are disputed, is in the next section.

      Diversity

      The range of people in a workforce or community, and valuing those differences (Acas). Describes who is present, not how they are treated.

      Equality

      Equal opportunity and fair treatment, and not treating people unfairly because of a protected characteristic. In UK law this is the frame of the Equality Act 2010 and the public sector equality duty.

      Equity

      Giving people what they need so that they have fair chances, because people do not all start from the same place. Some definitions extend this to equal outcomes, which is where the term is most disputed. "Equity" is not itself a statutory term in the UK equality duties cited here.

      Inclusion (and belonging)

      Everyone feels valued, safe to contribute and able to thrive. University and professional-body definitions frame inclusion as a sense of belonging.

      Accessibility

      Making services, content and workplaces usable by disabled people. For public sector websites and apps it is a legal requirement: WCAG 2.2 AA plus an accessibility statement. In employment it links to the Equality Act duty to make reasonable adjustments.
