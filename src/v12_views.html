Getting the tone right

Joy and remembrance, side by side

Celebrate loudly

Food, music, stories, achievements, jokes and the jollof debate. Black history is full of joy, invention and community, and it deserves a party.

Remember gently

Give slavery, colonial violence, racist killings and the Windrush scandal their own quiet moment. Don't put them next to games or giveaways, and warn people before showing distressing material.

Let people opt in

Some colleagues carry this history personally. Make attendance optional, offer a quiet room and signpost support, such as your employee assistance programme.

Follow the lead of those affected

Ask your staff network and community partners how they want ancestors and loss to be marked. Different communities do this differently.

Food and heritage

Pull up a chair

Nothing says "community" like a shared pot. Food travelled with African people everywhere they went, by choice and by force, and kept memory, language and family alive. Here are dishes from across Africa and the diaspora, the ingredients behind them, and home-style recipes to cook for your team. Every family does it differently, and every family is sure theirs is right.

No network yet?

Starting one in your organisation

  1. Find a senior sponsor and agree time for the chair and committee in their objectives.
  2. Write short terms of reference: purpose, membership (open to allies), decision-making and data protection.
  3. Ask your HR or EDI team for a budget line and a channel in your collaboration tools.
  4. Connect with cross-government groups such as the Civil Service Race Forum so you don't start from scratch.
  5. Agree with leaders how the network's advice will be answered, and publish the answers.
