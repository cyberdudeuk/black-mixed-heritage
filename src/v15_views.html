Stereotypes

Common stereotypes, and what the evidence shows

A stereotype is a generalisation about a whole group. Even when it seems harmless, it shapes how people are judged at work, at school and in services. We name each one briefly, then set out sourced evidence and its limits.

              For managers and recruiters
              Positive action and the law
              The Equality Act 2010 lets employers take proportionate steps to help under-represented groups. It does not allow positive discrimination.
              
    
              
      
              Section 158: general positive action
              Lawful where you reasonably think people who share a protected characteristic are disadvantaged, have different needs or are under-represented, and the step is proportionate. Examples: targeted outreach, application workshops, mentoring and development programmes.
              Sourced · S93
              
      
              Section 159: recruitment and promotion
              You may treat someone more favourably only if they are as qualified as the other candidate, the group is under-represented or disadvantaged, and you do not have a policy of always doing so. Take HR and legal advice first.
